Tabeebi

Healthcare booking platform in the UAE

Design, mobile, web, backend, data pipeline

Live in all seven emirates

Tabeebi is two products that must never disagree. Patients find a verified specialist and hold a real appointment in a couple of taps. Clinics run their doctors, their front desk, and every branch calendar from one dashboard. I built both sides, and the layer underneath that keeps them true to each other.

01Where a booking system usually breaks

Provider listings arrived from a dozen sources, and no two agreed on a phone number. Two patients could tap the same slot in the same second. Every screen had to read naturally in English and in Arabic, in opposite directions. Any one of the three, left alone, makes the app lie to somebody.

Raw listings · 3 sources
  • Al Noor Clinic+971 50 123 4567Source A
  • Al Noor Clinic0501234567Duplicate
  • AL-NOOR CLINIC971501234567Source C
  • Al Noor Clinic+971 50 123 4567 · Deira, DubaiDeduped nightly

02Solution

A nightly pipeline folds every source into one clean record per clinic. Redis serves availability instantly, and the write path locks the slot the moment it is taken, so the second tap loses cleanly instead of double booking. The interface was built bidirectional from day one, so it flips language without breaking a pixel.

03Patient app · the side that has to feel effortless

Patients do not care how the calendar works. They care that the slot is real, that the reminder arrives, and that the family’s visits live in one place.

  • Book in a couple of tapsSearch by name, location, or symptom, then hold a real slot on the spot. No phone call, no hold music, no callback to confirm.
  • Reminders that reach youA nudge at 48 and 24 hours, plus a final one the patient sets, anywhere from 30 minutes to 4 hours out. Email and push, so a visit never quietly slips.
  • The whole family, one accountBook for the people you care for, and keep every visit in one timeline, whether the patient booked it or the clinic did.

04Clinic dashboard · the side that has to be right

A patient sees one open slot. Behind it sit a doctor’s real day, a secretary’s queue, an owner’s staff list, and every branch calendar, and all of them have to agree. Six views onto one calendar. Nobody opens a screen they do not need.

  • Run the front deskSecretaries confirm bookings, add new patients, and keep every doctor’s day moving from a single screen.
  • A focused day for doctorsEach doctor opens Tabeebi straight to their own schedule and patient list, with nothing else in the way.
  • Manage your whole teamOwners add or remove staff and approve the requests from doctors and secretaries who want to join.
  • Schedules that stay liveAvailability, days off, and visit lengths update in real time across web and mobile.
  • Every branch in one systemRun one clinic or ten, with bookings, queues, and records in step across all of them.
  • One record per patientEvery appointment, note, and prescription lands in a single patient timeline the whole team can see.

05Result

Tabeebi is live across all seven emirates, on iOS, Android, and the web. Patients book, and their family’s visits stay in one account. Clinics run bookings, queues, and records for one branch or for ten. Both sides read the same calendar.

helloMuller is the award-winning brand identity studio of Tom Muller, behind visual identities for Marvel, DC, Sony Pictures and Google. The site has one job: put the work first. One colour, one typeface, and a ledger of projects that opens row by row.

01A quiet frame for loud work

A studio whose work is loud needs a frame that stays quiet. Every page is the studio orange with black type, and nothing else competes. Imagery appears only when a visitor asks for it, so the identity work is never fighting the site’s own design for attention.

02The ledger

The home page lists featured projects as rows: title, tagline, client, services. A row expands in place into the story and the pieces, then closes again without leaving the page. Try it with the studio’s own featured projects, and filter the rows by service.

    03The catalogue

    A growing archive of projects the studio has undertaken: 67 projects catalogued on one page, in the same row system as the home page, so the archive reads like the rest of the site instead of a separate database.

    04Every screen

    The same orange, type and rows from a wide desktop down to a phone. Nothing is hidden on mobile. The rows stack, the type scales, and the ledger still opens in place. Drag the viewport to see the layout change.

    Job Pull

    Hiring-signal engine for a software agency

    Client
    Software agency, internal tool
    Built

    Stack

    Runs on

    My role
    Design, build, deploy

    Job Pull watches LinkedIn’s public job listings for companies hiring the kind of engineers an agency sells, and turns them into a ranked list of people to call. A company with four open backend roles is telling you where its team is short. I designed and built all of it: the scraper, the store, the enrichment worker, the operator’s panel, and the pipeline that ships it.

    01Hiring is the buying signal

    An agency that sells engineering capacity needs to know who is short of it this week. The job posting is the evidence. The company is the lead. So the product is not a job board: it is a list of companies sorted by how many roles they have open, with the right person at each one.

    An operator saves a search once, a runner, and presses Run, or gives it a schedule. Everything else happens in the panel. Nobody opens a terminal.

    Every company page answers the same three questions: how many roles are open, who is reachable, and what the tool already knows. The demo data on these screens is invented; the real store holds real contacts.

    02Ten requests, not ten thousand

    The automated side never signs in. No account, no cookies, nothing to ban. It reads the same public pages anyone can, slowly: four to nine seconds between requests, a daily allowance per proxy, a proxy taken out the moment it is blocked, and a run that stops rather than fall back to the home connection. Keeping each run small is the whole rate-limit strategy. Toggle what a run fetches and watch its cost.

    One run · 50 postings95
    • Search pages 5
    • Job details 50
    • Company pages 40
    Cold cache and full details: the most a run of 50 costs.Measured: 53 requests first run, 3 on the repeat

    The proxies page shows each exit’s health and what it has spent today. When one earns a 429 it rests on its own while the others carry the run.

    03A monitor, not a scraper

    Every card a runner sees is stored as a sighting. That history answers what a list cannot: who started hiring this week, and which role just closed. A closed role usually means it was filled, and a team that just hired is a team onboarding. It is the strongest claim the tool makes, so it is the one it must never invent.

    One absence proves nothing: a posting once returned a 404 and came back eight minutes later. So a role closes only after three misses in a row, by the runner that used to see it. A blocked run closes nothing. It did not look, it failed.

      04From a company to a name

      A background worker takes each run’s new companies and works out the rest without touching LinkedIn. The website, accepted only when the site itself proves it. Named people from the team page, public filings and the GitHub organisation. The email format, settled from commit authors and Gravatar. Every step has its own daily budget and stops at the first challenge instead of working around it.

      • Direct or agencyEvery posting is classed as the hiring company or a recruiter, so nobody pitches a staffing agency by mistake.
      • A queue with reasonsThe Pipeline ranks who to call next by signal, and every rank shows the reasons behind it.
      • Why now, in one lineA small language model writes one line per lead from the posting and the company’s state, in the agency’s call-sheet style.
      • Straight into the dialerA run downloads straight into the calling tool’s contact-import template.

      05Shipped like a product

      Sign-in, saved runners on a schedule, live run logs, proxy health and exports all live in one panel that follows the system theme. Every push runs the test suite inside the Docker image, publishes an immutable tag and rolls it out to a k3s cluster behind TLS. Thirty-seven design notes, including a decision log, explain why each part is the way it is.

      The panel follows your system theme
      • 608 testsEach one gets an empty PostgreSQL schema of its own and drops it afterwards, so the suite never touches real data.
      • One run at a timeA machine-wide lock serialises runs from the panel, the scheduler and the command line alike.
      • Partial is never completeA hard block ends the run and marks it, rather than writing half a result as if it were whole.
